How DTF printing works

DTF stands for Direct to Film. The design is printed onto a film with an adhesive backing, then transferred to the garment using heat and pressure.

It works well for multicolor logos, illustrations and gradients on many cotton, polyester and blended garments. We check the exact fabric, color and artwork before recommending a setup.

Choose the garment for the job

  • T-shirts for events and merch

    Soft crewnecks give illustrations and event graphics room on the front or back.

  • Polos and performance shirts

    A small left-chest logo works well for uniforms. A larger back print can help identify an outdoor crew.

  • Hoodies for teams and staff

    Use the chest or back for your design. We check how the hood, seams and pocket affect placement.

Get your artwork ready

Send a high-resolution PNG with a transparent background when possible. If you have the original artwork, include it so we can check small text, fine lines and any background that should stay unprinted.

Tell us where the artwork should go: left chest, center front or back. Include the approximate width and height if you know them. We’ll review the scale and placement in your digital mockup.

Common DTF printing questions

Can I print on light and dark garments?

Yes. We can print on many light and dark garments. We review contrast, fabric and artwork together before preparing the mockup.

Can the same shirt have a chest logo and a back print?

Yes. Include both positions and both files in your request so we can quote the complete setup.

Can I supply my own garments?

Ask us before buying or dropping off blanks. Customer-supplied garments require approval after we review the fabric and construction.

How do I wash DTF-printed apparel?

Wash inside out in cold water and avoid ironing directly over the print. Follow the garment’s care label for drying and ironing.
